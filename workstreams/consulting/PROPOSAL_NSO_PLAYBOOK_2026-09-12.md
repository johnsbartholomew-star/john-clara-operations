Hello,

Your goal is not simply to collect existing checklists—it is to turn scattered functional knowledge into a Store Leader operating model that clearly shows what happens, when it happens, who owns it, what it depends on, and when a decision or escalation is required. That strongly matches my operating background.

I have led retail teams and operations across Cole Haan, Journeys, Target, and Dr. Martens, with responsibility spanning staffing, recruiting, workforce planning, training, inventory control, loss prevention, daily execution, and P&L performance. My experience is operator-led: I focus on whether a store leader and team can actually use the process under real operating conditions.

For the Phase 1 pilot, I would:

1. Inventory the existing materials and map each item to the NSO timeline and intended owner.
2. Interview the experienced operators around decisions, failure points, handoffs, exceptions, and lessons missing from formal documents.
3. Build the Store Leader architecture around milestones, workstreams, dependencies, ownership, risks, and escalation triggers.
4. Validate the structure against both opening experiences, separating universal requirements from location-specific exceptions.
5. Develop one sample chapter and checklist that show how the full playbook would work in practice.

I can share an illustrative retail-opening checklist that demonstrates my approach to ownership, readiness checks, exceptions, and manager sign-off. It is demonstration work rather than a prior client engagement, and I would tailor the pilot output to your actual materials and environment.

Before finalizing the milestone plan, I would confirm the approximate volume of source materials, preferred deliverable format, and whether the master timeline should begin at site approval, lease execution, or another internal trigger.
